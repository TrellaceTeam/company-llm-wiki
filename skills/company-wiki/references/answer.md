# Answer

Answer a question from the wiki, so the answer rests on sourced, reviewed pages the person can check.

## 1. Find the pages

Read the wiki's index, open the folder indexes that fit, and read the pages whose questions match. Search the wiki for names and terms the indexes miss.

**Done when** you have read every page that bears on the question, or you know the wiki has none.

## 2. Check what matters

For each claim the person will act on, such as a number, a date, a commitment or a decision, open its source and confirm it. Note anything that weakens a page you use: an item under "Open questions", an "Out of date after" date that has passed, or "Kept for history". The index says when it was last updated and how often it updates. When the last update is older than two of those cycles, say the wiki may be behind.

**Done when** each claim you'll give is confirmed in its source or flagged.

## 3. Answer

Lead with the answer. Link each page you used, and quote a source only where the exact words matter. Say plainly what the wiki doesn't cover. Then search the source folders for it, and only those, and report what you find as not yet in the wiki.

When the answer joins several pages into something a teammate would ask again, such as a comparison or a timeline, offer to save it as a page. With a yes, write it by the page rules, citing the same sources, and add it to its folder index.

When the person says your answer is wrong, follow [fix.md](fix.md).

**Done when** the answer names its pages, flags what's uncertain, and states any gap.
